## What are "non-covered" prices and why are they public?

Korea's national health insurance covers most standard treatment for residents. Items outside that coverage — premium materials, many vision corrections, some imaging and cosmetic care — are called **non-covered (비급여)** and are paid entirely by the patient. To help patients compare, the government requires hospital-level institutions to report these fees to the **Health Insurance Review & Assessment Service (HIRA)**, which publishes them as open data.

For international visitors this matters twice: short-term visitors are not covered by Korean insurance, so you pay self-pay prices for everything, and the published list is the most objective way to see what a hospital normally charges.

## How to read a published price

Each record tells you the hospital, the item (for example "dental implant, per tooth, zirconia"), the amount in Korean won and the date the price took effect. On our [hospital pages](/{lang}/hospitals) we show:

- **From** — the lowest price the hospital lists for that item
- **Published range** — when a hospital lists several variants (brands, materials), the lowest and highest
- **Reference date** — when the hospital reported the price

> **Tip:** A wide range at one hospital usually reflects different materials or brands. Ask which variant your quote is based on.

## Why the price you are quoted can differ

- **Package contents.** Quotes often bundle tests, anesthesia, medication and follow-up visits.
- **International patient services.** Interpretation, coordination and documents in English can add cost.
- **Clinical findings.** The final plan depends on examination; a bone graft or extra imaging may be needed.
- **Clinics vs hospitals.** Small clinics (의원) are not covered by the same disclosure data as hospital-level institutions, so their prices may not appear.
- **Cosmetic procedures.** Purely cosmetic treatments (fillers, botulinum toxin, eyelid surgery) are generally outside this disclosure, so compare written quotes instead.

## What a good written quote includes

1. Diagnosis or reason for treatment, and the exact item names
2. Materials or product brand (implant system, lens model)
3. Every included step: exams, imaging, anesthesia, medication, follow-ups
4. Items that might be added and their price
5. Payment schedule, deposit and refund policy
6. Validity date of the quote

Compare it with the hospital's published price. If the difference is large, ask why — a clear answer is a good sign.

## Paying for treatment

Most hospitals accept major international credit cards; some require a deposit when booking surgery. Keep the itemized receipt (진료비 세부산정내역) and ask for an English medical certificate if you plan to claim from travel or international insurance.

## FAQ

### Where do the prices on this site come from?

From the Health Insurance Review & Assessment Service (HIRA), which publishes the self-pay prices hospitals report. Each price on our hospital pages shows its reference date and source.

### Is the published price what I will pay?

It is a reference. Your final price depends on the exact treatment plan, materials and what is included. Ask for a written quote and compare it with the published price.

### Why can't I find prices for cosmetic procedures?

The public disclosure system focuses on medically necessary non-covered items and generally excludes purely cosmetic procedures, so those prices must be confirmed with each clinic.

### Are prices cheaper in Busan than in Seoul?

It depends on the hospital and item. Published ranges overlap widely between the two cities, so compare specific hospitals rather than cities.
