## How Korean restaurants work

A few habits make your first meals much easier:

- **Banchan are free.** The small side dishes (kimchi, seasoned vegetables) come with your meal and are refilled on request.
- **Call staff politely.** Press the table bell if there is one, or say "jeogiyo!" (excuse me).
- **Order minimums.** Barbecue and some stew restaurants ask for at least two portions.
- **Pay at the counter** when you leave; tipping is not part of the culture.
- **Water is self-service** in many casual places.
- **Kiosks and tablet menus** are common and often have an English option.

## 20 dishes to try

### Easy favorites

1. **Bibimbap** — rice with vegetables, egg and chili paste; mix before eating
2. **Bulgogi** — sweet soy-marinated beef, mild and kid-friendly
3. **Japchae** — glass noodles stir-fried with vegetables
4. **Korean fried chicken** — crispy, often with a sweet-spicy glaze; best with friends
5. **Gimbap** — seaweed rice rolls, ideal for day trips

### Korean barbecue

6. **Samgyeopsal** — grilled pork belly wrapped in lettuce with garlic and ssamjang
7. **Galbi** — marinated beef or pork ribs
8. **Dak-galbi** — spicy chicken stir-fried at the table

### Soups and stews

9. **Samgyetang** — whole young chicken in ginseng broth, popular in summer
10. **Gukbap** — rice in hot broth; Busan's pork version (dwaeji gukbap) is famous
11. **Kimchi jjigae** — kimchi and pork stew
12. **Sundubu jjigae** — soft tofu stew, spice level often adjustable
13. **Naengmyeon** — cold buckwheat noodles, refreshing in summer

### Market and street food

14. **Tteokbokki** — chewy rice cakes in sweet-spicy sauce
15. **Bindaetteok** — crispy mung bean pancakes (famous at Gwangjang Market)
16. **Hotteok** — sweet filled pancakes; Busan's version is stuffed with seeds
17. **Eomuk** — fish cake skewers in broth, a winter staple
18. **Mayak gimbap** — small addictive gimbap rolls

### Something different

19. **Jeon** — savory pancakes, perfect on a rainy day with makgeolli
20. **Hoe** — fresh raw fish, best at fish markets such as Busan's Jagalchi

## Spice levels, allergies and diets

| Need | What to know |
|---|---|
| Mild food | Ask "an maepge" (not spicy). Bulgogi, galbi, japchae and samgyetang are usually mild. |
| Vegetarian | Many dishes use meat or fish stock; temple food restaurants and some bibimbap places are good options. |
| Halal | Itaewon has the largest concentration of halal restaurants in Seoul. |
| Allergies | Peanut, shellfish and wheat appear in sauces; show a written allergy card in Korean. |

> **Tip:** After a medical procedure, ask your doctor what to avoid. Spicy food, alcohol and very hot soups are common restrictions for a few days. Soft options include juk (rice porridge), steamed egg and samgyetang.

## Table etiquette in one minute

- Wait for the eldest to start eating if you're dining with Koreans.
- Don't stick chopsticks upright in rice.
- Pour drinks for others and accept with two hands when an elder pours for you.
- Lift the soup spoon, not the bowl.

## FAQ

### What Korean food should first-time visitors try?

Bibimbap, bulgogi, Korean barbecue (samgyeopsal or galbi), japchae, fried chicken, tteokbokki and a soup such as samgyetang or gukbap give a good overview from mild to spicy.

### Do you tip in Korean restaurants?

No. Tipping is not expected; prices include service. Pay at the counter when you leave.

### Is Korean food always spicy?

No. Many dishes are mild — bulgogi, galbi, japchae, samgyetang and gimbap. Ask "an maepge" for no spice where possible.

### Are side dishes free in Korea?

Yes. Banchan come with your meal and can usually be refilled for free.
