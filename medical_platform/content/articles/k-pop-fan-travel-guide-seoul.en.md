## What can a K-pop fan actually do in Seoul?

More than you might expect — but much of it depends on timing. Comebacks, music show schedules and concerts change weekly, so build your trip around a few fixed experiences and leave room for spontaneous ones.

![K-Star Road in Apgujeong, Gangnam](https://tong.visitkorea.or.kr/cms/resource/09/3565309_image2_1.jpg "Korea Tourism Organization")

## Weekly TV music shows

Korea's music shows are where groups perform new songs during promotion periods. A typical weekly schedule:

| Day | Show | Broadcaster |
|---|---|---|
| Wednesday | Show Champion | MBC M |
| Thursday | M Countdown | Mnet |
| Friday | Music Bank | KBS |
| Saturday | Show! Music Core | MBC |
| Sunday | Inkigayo | SBS |

Schedules shift for holidays and special broadcasts. **Most audience places are allocated to official fan-club members** through the artist's fan community, sometimes with pre-recording sessions early in the morning. A small number of general audience places may be offered by broadcasters — check the show's official notices. Even without a ticket, fans often gather outside to see artists arrive (please keep to the areas staff allow).

## Concerts and venues

Large concerts in Seoul commonly take place at **KSPO Dome** and other halls in Olympic Park, and at **Gocheok Sky Dome**. Smaller showcases and festivals happen at university festivals, Olympic Park and Han River parks in spring and autumn.

> **Tip:** Buy tickets only from the official ticketing partner announced by the artist's agency. Many Korean ticketing sites have global versions with English support. Never pay strangers on social media for tickets.

## Neighborhoods to explore

### Gangnam and Apgujeong: K-Star Road

A street in Apgujeong Rodeo lined with "GangnamDol" bear statues decorated for K-pop groups. It is free and easy to combine with cafés and shopping in Cheongdam and Sinsa.

### Hongdae: street performances and dance culture

Evenings and weekends bring busking and dance covers to the streets around Hongik University. It's also where you'll find many K-pop dance studios that run one-off classes for tourists — learn a chorus routine in an hour or two.

### Myeongdong and Hongdae: albums and merchandise

Record and merchandise shops in Myeongdong and Hongdae stock albums, photocards and light sticks. Pop-up stores tied to new releases open regularly in Seongsu and large malls — follow artists' official accounts.

## A sample two-day K-pop itinerary

1. **Day 1 morning:** K-Star Road and cafés in Apgujeong and Sinsa
2. **Day 1 afternoon:** album and merchandise shopping in Myeongdong
3. **Day 1 evening:** Han River park picnic and city lights
4. **Day 2 morning:** a K-pop dance class in Hongdae
5. **Day 2 afternoon:** pop-up stores in Seongsu
6. **Day 2 evening:** street performances in Hongdae, or a concert if you have tickets

## Fan etiquette that locals appreciate

- Don't follow artists' cars or wait at private homes or dormitories.
- Respect "no photography" rules at music shows and events.
- Queue patiently — lines for pop-ups and fan events are part of the culture.
- Keep noise down in residential streets around studios and companies.

## FAQ

### Can tourists attend a K-pop music show recording?

Sometimes, but most audience places go to official fan-club members through the artist's fan community. Check each broadcaster's notices for general audience applications and arrive very early.

### Where are big K-pop concerts held in Seoul?

Commonly at KSPO Dome and other halls in Olympic Park, and at Gocheok Sky Dome. Check the official announcement for each tour.

### What is K-Star Road?

A street in Apgujeong Rodeo, Gangnam, with bear statues decorated for K-pop groups. It's free to visit and popular for photos.

### Where can I buy K-pop albums in Seoul?

Record and merchandise shops in Myeongdong and Hongdae, plus pop-up stores in Seongsu and major malls during new releases.
