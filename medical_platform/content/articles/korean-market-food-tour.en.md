## Why markets are the best place to taste Korea

Traditional markets combine cheap, freshly made food with a slice of everyday life. You can sit at a counter next to locals, point at what looks good and try several dishes for the price of one restaurant meal.

## Seoul

### Gwangjang Market — the classic

![Gwangjang Market food alley](https://tong.visitkorea.or.kr/cms/resource/46/4135946_image2_1.jpg "Korea Tourism Organization")

One of Seoul's oldest markets, famous for its central food alley.

- **Bindaetteok:** mung bean pancakes ground and fried to order
- **Mayak gimbap:** bite-sized gimbap with mustard sauce
- **Yukhoe:** seasoned raw beef, a local specialty
- **Kalguksu and mandu:** hand-cut noodles and dumplings

### Tongin Market — the coin lunch box

Near Gyeongbokgung Palace. Buy brass coins (yeopjeon) at the market café, pick up a lunch box and fill it with small portions from participating stalls. Don't miss **gireum tteokbokki**, stir-fried rice cakes in oil and chili.

### Mangwon Market — local and relaxed

A neighborhood market in Mapo popular with young locals, a short walk from the Han River. Good for croquettes, fried chicken and fruit to take on a riverside picnic.

![Mangwon Market](https://tong.visitkorea.or.kr/cms/resource/11/3509911_image2_1.jpg "Korea Tourism Organization")

### Namdaemun Market — shopping and snacks

Seoul's largest traditional market, close to Myeongdong. Try **vegetable hotteok** and the **kalguksu alley**, where many stalls add a small bonus bowl of naengmyeon or barley rice to your order.

## Busan

### Jagalchi Market — the seafood market

![Jagalchi Market on Busan's waterfront](https://tong.visitkorea.or.kr/cms/resource_photo/05/4108305_image2_1.JPG "Korea Tourism Organization")

Korea's best-known fish market, on the Nampo waterfront. Choose live seafood on the ground floor and take it to restaurants upstairs to be prepared as hoe (sliced raw fish), grilled or in a spicy stew (maeuntang), for a table charge.

- Ask the price per kilogram before choosing.
- Confirm the preparation fee and side dishes upstairs.
- Combine with BIFF Square nearby for **ssiat hotteok** (seed-filled pancakes) and Gukje Market.

## Practical tips

| Topic | Advice |
|---|---|
| Best time | Lunch (11:30–14:00) or early evening; some stalls close on Sundays or certain days |
| Payment | Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for small stalls |
| Sharing | Order one dish per person and share to taste more |
| Hygiene | Choose busy stalls with high turnover |
| After treatment | Raw dishes (hoe, yukhoe) may be restricted after procedures — ask your doctor |

## A half-day route in Seoul

1. Late morning at **Tongin Market** (coin lunch box)
2. Walk or take a short taxi to **Gyeongbokgung Palace** and **Bukchon**
3. Late afternoon snack crawl at **Gwangjang Market**
4. Evening walk along **Cheonggyecheon Stream**

## FAQ

### Which market in Seoul is best for food?

Gwangjang Market is the classic choice for bindaetteok, mayak gimbap and yukhoe. Tongin Market is the most unique experience with its coin lunch box.

### How does Jagalchi Market work?

Choose live seafood from vendors on the ground floor, then have it prepared at a restaurant upstairs for a table and cooking fee. Ask prices first.

### Do Korean markets accept credit cards?

Most do, but small stalls may prefer cash. Carry some won for snacks.
