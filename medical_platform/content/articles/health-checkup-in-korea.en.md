## Why get screened in Korea?

Health screening is part of everyday life in Korea, and large hospitals run dedicated check-up centers that move patients through dozens of tests in a few hours. For visitors, the attraction is a thorough examination in one visit, specialists on site if something is found, and the ability to combine it with a short trip.

The tertiary hospitals in our [check-up category](/{lang}/hospitals?specialty=health_checkup) publish prices for imaging such as MRI and ultrasound, so you can see the cost of common add-ons before you book.

## What does a typical package include?

Packages vary, but most comprehensive programs combine the following:

| Area | Common tests |
|---|---|
| Basics | Height, weight, body composition, blood pressure, vision, hearing |
| Lab tests | Blood count, liver and kidney function, lipids, blood sugar, thyroid, tumor markers, urine |
| Imaging | Chest X-ray, abdominal ultrasound, thyroid or breast ultrasound |
| Heart | ECG; echocardiography in premium packages |
| Digestive | Upper endoscopy (gastroscopy), often colonoscopy in premium packages |
| Women's health | Pap smear, pelvic ultrasound, mammography |
| Add-ons | Brain MRI/MRA, low-dose chest CT, coronary CT, bone density |

> **Tip:** Ask for a package description in your language and check which tests are optional. It is easier to add a test before the day than to schedule it afterwards.

## How much do add-on tests cost?

Self-pay imaging prices published by Seoul and Busan tertiary hospitals (September 2026, lowest listed price per hospital):

| Test | Lowest | Median | Highest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brain MRI (standard) | ₩553,000 | ₩815,000 | ₩1,045,000 |
| Thyroid ultrasound | ₩62,000 | ₩180,000 | ₩266,000 |
| Breast ultrasound | ₩124,000 | ₩242,000 | ₩330,000 |
| Upper abdominal ultrasound | ₩128,000 | ₩222,120 | ₩313,000 |

Package prices themselves are set by each hospital and are not part of the public disclosure, so request a quote. See [how Korean medical prices work](/{lang}/magazine/korea-medical-prices-explained).

## How to book

1. Choose two or three hospitals and email their international centers with your age, sex, medical history and the tests you want.
2. Ask for the package list, price, available dates and preparation instructions.
3. Book two to four weeks ahead — popular dates fill up, especially for sedated endoscopy and MRI.
4. If you want everything coordinated in your language, you can [request a free consultation](/{lang}/consult).

## How to prepare

- **Fasting:** usually nothing to eat after dinner the day before (8–12 hours). Water is often allowed until a set time — follow the hospital's instructions.
- **Colonoscopy:** you will drink a bowel preparation solution the evening before and early that morning; plan to stay at your accommodation.
- **Medication:** tell the hospital about blood thinners, diabetes medication and supplements in advance.
- **Documents:** passport, previous results, and a list of medications and allergies.

## On the day

Expect to arrive early in the morning, change into a gown and follow a guide or color-coded route between stations. Sedated endoscopy is usually done at the end; you will need 30–60 minutes of recovery and **should not drive or sign important documents** for the rest of the day. Many centers serve a light meal afterwards.

## Results

Some results are explained by a doctor the same afternoon. Full reports, including pathology from biopsies, typically arrive within one to two weeks, and many hospitals can send an English summary by email or post. If something needs follow-up, the hospital can book a specialist — ask whether that is possible during your stay.

## FAQ

### How long does a health check-up in Korea take?

Most comprehensive check-ups take half a day to a full day. Premium packages with MRI, CT or colonoscopy may need a second visit or a full day.

### Can I get my results in English?

Many university hospitals with international centers provide an English summary report. Ask when booking, since some reports are only issued in Korean by default.

### Is sedated endoscopy safe?

It is commonly performed in Korea under monitoring. You must fast, arrange to rest afterwards and avoid driving for the rest of the day. Discuss your health conditions with the hospital in advance.

### How far ahead should I book?

Two to four weeks is a good guide, longer for peak seasons or if you need MRI and sedated endoscopy on the same day.
