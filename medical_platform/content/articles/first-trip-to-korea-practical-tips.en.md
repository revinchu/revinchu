## Before you fly

1. **Check entry rules.** Depending on your nationality you may need a visa, a K-ETA (electronic travel authorization) or neither. Rules and temporary exemptions change — check the official K-ETA and visa portal sites before booking.
2. **Passport validity.** Make sure your passport is valid for your whole stay; you'll also need it for tax refunds and some hotel check-ins.
3. **Travel insurance.** Short-term visitors are not covered by Korean health insurance. If you're coming for treatment, check what your policy covers.
4. **Connectivity.** Buy an eSIM or a prepaid SIM, or rent a pocket Wi-Fi at the airport.
5. **Power.** Korea uses 220V with two round pins (type C/F).

## Getting around

6. **T-money card.** A rechargeable card for subways, buses and many taxis and convenience stores. Buy and top up at convenience stores or station machines. Tap in and out on buses to get transfer discounts.
7. **Unlimited passes.** Seoul offers a short-term unlimited transit pass for visitors (the Climate Card); check current options and where it's valid.
8. **Subway.** Signs are in Korean and English; station numbers make navigation easy. Trains are clean, safe and frequent.
9. **Airport to city.** From Incheon Airport, the AREX train and airport limousine buses reach central Seoul; Gimhae Airport serves Busan.
10. **Seoul to Busan.** The KTX high-speed train takes roughly two and a half to three hours. Book on the official railway site or app.
11. **Taxis.** Call with Kakao T; international cards can often be registered. Have your destination in Korean.

## Apps that make life easier

| App | Use |
|---|---|
| Naver Map / KakaoMap | Directions, transit, walking routes (more accurate than Google Maps in Korea) |
| Papago | Translation, including camera translation of menus |
| Kakao T | Taxis |
| Official ticketing apps | Concerts, KTX trains |
| Delivery apps | Food delivery to your hotel (Korean phone number often needed) |

> **Tip:** Save your hotel's and hospital's addresses in Korean script in your notes — showing it to a taxi driver is the fastest way to get there.

## Money and shopping

12. **Cards** are accepted almost everywhere, including taxis and markets; carry a little cash for small stalls.
13. **ATMs** marked "Global" accept foreign cards; convenience store ATMs are easiest.
14. **No tipping.** Service is included.
15. **Tax refunds.** Show your passport at Tax Free shops; many deduct tax at checkout, otherwise claim at the airport. See our [K-beauty shopping guide](/{lang}/magazine/k-beauty-shopping-guide-seoul).
16. **Convenience stores** are everywhere and open late or 24 hours — great for snacks, T-money top-ups and basics.

## Health and safety

17. **Emergency numbers:** 119 (fire and ambulance), 112 (police).
18. **1330 Korea Travel Hotline:** 24-hour multilingual help for travelers.
19. **Pharmacies** (약국) sell common medicines; look for the green cross sign.
20. **Medical care:** for planned treatment, read [how to get medical treatment in Korea](/{lang}/magazine/medical-treatment-in-korea-guide).
21. **Seasons:** summers are hot and humid with a rainy season; winters are cold and dry. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable.
22. **Fine dust:** on high-dust days, locals wear masks — check the air quality in weather apps.

## Etiquette in a nutshell

23. Take off shoes where you see a step up or a shoe rack (homes, some restaurants, temples).
24. Keep voices low on the subway and use priority seats only if you need them.
25. Use both hands or touch your arm when giving or receiving something from an elder.

## Where to stay

For a medical trip, stay near your hospital for the first nights. For sightseeing, Myeongdong, Jongno and Hongdae in Seoul and Haeundae or Seomyeon in Busan are convenient bases. Our [concierge page](/{lang}/concierge) lists hotels and Korean restaurants by area.

## FAQ

### Do I need a K-ETA to visit Korea?

It depends on your nationality and current rules, which include temporary exemptions for some countries. Check the official K-ETA site before you travel.

### Does Google Maps work in Korea?

Google Maps shows public transit, but its walking and driving directions are limited in Korea. Locals and most visitors use Naver Map or KakaoMap.

### What is T-money and where can I buy it?

T-money is a rechargeable transport card for subways, buses and some taxis. Buy and top it up at convenience stores and subway stations.

### What number should I call for help in Korea?

Call 1330 for 24-hour multilingual travel help, 119 for ambulance and fire, and 112 for police.
