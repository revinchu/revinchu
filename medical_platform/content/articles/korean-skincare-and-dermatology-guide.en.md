## The Korean approach to skin, in plain terms

The famous "10-step routine" is more marketing than daily reality. Most people in Korea follow a simpler logic:

1. **Cleanse gently** — often an oil or balm cleanser at night, then a mild, low-pH foam
2. **Hydrate in layers** — a toner or essence, then a serum suited to their concern
3. **Seal it in** — a moisturizer matched to the season
4. **Protect every day** — sunscreen, reapplied when outdoors

The goal is a healthy skin barrier: hydrated, calm and protected. Many visitors find the most useful souvenir is not an exotic ingredient but a sunscreen and moisturizer they actually enjoy using. See the [K-beauty shopping guide](/{lang}/magazine/k-beauty-shopping-guide-seoul) for where to buy.

## How dermatology clinics work in Korea

Most cosmetic dermatology in Korea is done at private clinics (의원) rather than hospitals. They typically offer:

- a short consultation with a doctor or a counselor
- a treatment menu (lasers, skin boosters, peels, injectables)
- packages of several sessions, often with discounts

Two terms help you choose:

| You see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Clinic name includes **피부과** (e.g. "OO피부과의원") | Run by a board-certified dermatology specialist — only specialists may use the specialty in the official name |
| "진료과목: 피부과" on the sign | The clinic offers dermatology as a treatment subject, but the doctor may specialize in another field |

> **Tip:** Ask directly, "Who will perform my treatment — the doctor or a staff member?" In Korea, medical procedures such as lasers and injections must be performed by licensed medical professionals.

## Common treatments visitors ask about

| Treatment type | What it's for | Typical downtime |
|---|---|---|
| Laser toning / pigment lasers | Uneven tone, pigmentation | Little to a few days of redness |
| Fractional lasers | Texture, acne scars | Several days of redness and peeling |
| Radiofrequency / ultrasound lifting | Firmness | Usually minimal |
| Skin boosters and hydrating injections | Hydration, fine texture | Small bumps or bruises for a few days |
| Chemical peels | Dullness, mild acne | A few days of flaking |

Results and downtime vary by skin type and settings. Darker skin tones can be more prone to pigmentation after lasers — mention this at consultation.

## Questions to ask before you book

- Is the treating doctor a dermatology specialist? Will I see them in person?
- What exactly is included in the price (numbing cream, aftercare products, follow-up)?
- How many sessions are realistic, and how far apart?
- What should I avoid afterwards, and for how long?
- What happens if I have a reaction after I've flown home?

## Timing your trip

- Book consultation and treatment early in your stay, not the day before your flight.
- After lasers, avoid saunas (jjimjilbang), intense exercise and strong sun for several days.
- Long flights dry the skin — pack a gentle moisturizer and sunscreen in your carry-on.

## Prices and payment

Cosmetic treatments are generally outside Korea's public price disclosure, so prices vary widely between clinics and promotions. Get a written quote, check whether the price is per session or per package, and keep your receipt. ## FAQ

### What is the Korean skincare routine?

At its core: gentle cleansing, layered hydration, a moisturizer and daily sunscreen. The long multi-step routines are optional; most people use four or five products.

### How can I tell if a Korean clinic is run by a dermatologist?

In Korea, only board-certified specialists may include their specialty in the clinic's official name. A clinic named "OO피부과의원" is run by a dermatology specialist; a sign that only lists "진료과목: 피부과" means dermatology is offered but the doctor may specialize in another field.

### How long should I wait to fly after a laser treatment?

It depends on the treatment, but plan at least a few days before a long flight after stronger lasers, and follow the clinic's aftercare advice. Ask the doctor at consultation.

### Are cosmetic treatment prices published in Korea?

Generally not in the public non-covered price data, which focuses on medically necessary items. Compare written quotes from clinics.
