## Why do people travel to Korea for medical care?

Korea combines large, highly specialized hospitals with short waiting times and a dense network of clinics in Seoul and Busan. University hospitals such as those in the [hospital directory](/{lang}/hospitals) run dedicated international centers with coordinators and interpreters, and the country has a formal system for registering providers who treat foreign patients.

Another reason is transparency. Every hospital-level institution must report its self-pay (non-covered) fees to the **Health Insurance Review & Assessment Service (HIRA)**, so you can compare published prices before contacting anyone. We show those official prices — with the reference date — on each hospital page.

## Step 1: Decide what kind of care you need

Start with the treatment, not the city. Common reasons international visitors come to Korea include:

- **Health screenings** at large hospitals, often completed in one or two days
- **Dental work** such as implants and crowns, which usually needs two or more visits several months apart
- **Eye surgery** such as LASIK, LASEK or premium lens implants
- **Korean medicine** (acupuncture, pharmacopuncture, chuna therapy) for musculoskeletal pain
- **Dermatology and cosmetic procedures**, mostly at private clinics

> **Tip:** Treatments that need several visits (implants, orthodontics) are easier to plan if you can return to Korea, or if the hospital can coordinate follow-up with a dentist at home. Ask about this before you start.

## Step 2: Choose a hospital you can verify

Look for three things:

1. **Registration for international patients.** Korea's Medical Service Act requires providers who actively attract foreign patients to register. You can check registered institutions on the government's Medical Korea portal.
2. **The right level of hospital.** Tertiary (university) hospitals handle complex cases and comprehensive screenings; specialized hospitals (dental, eye, Korean medicine) focus on one field and are often faster to book.
3. **Published information.** Hospital type, number of doctors, address and self-pay prices are public data. Our [hospital pages](/{lang}/hospitals) show this information with its source.

| Hospital type | Good for | Typical booking |
|---|---|---|
| Tertiary / university hospital | Screenings, complex or multi-specialty care | International center by email |
| Dental hospital | Implants, crowns, gum treatment | Consultation, then staged visits |
| Eye hospital | LASIK/LASEK, cataract and lens surgery | Pre-op exam, surgery 1–2 days later |
| Korean medicine hospital | Back and joint pain, rehabilitation | Course of sessions over days or weeks |

## Step 3: Check prices and get a written quote

Published HIRA prices show what each hospital charges for self-pay items such as an implant, a brain MRI or a session of chuna therapy. Use them to understand the range in your city — then ask the hospital for a **written quote** covering:

- what is included (tests, anesthesia, medication, follow-up visits)
- what is not included (extra procedures, room upgrades)
- whether the price for international patients differs from the published price
- the cancellation and refund policy

Read our explainer on [how Korean medical prices work](/{lang}/magazine/korea-medical-prices-explained) for details.

## Step 4: Visas and entry

Many nationalities can enter Korea visa-free or with an electronic travel authorization (K-ETA) for short stays, which is usually enough for screenings or short procedures. For longer or repeated treatment, Korea issues a **medical tourism visa (C-3-3)** for stays of up to 90 days and a **G-1-10 visa** for longer treatment. Requirements change, so confirm on the official K-ETA and Korea Visa Portal sites for your nationality before booking flights.

## Step 5: Communication, interpreters and records

Large hospitals have international centers with English, Chinese, Japanese and other language staff. Smaller hospitals may rely on medical interpreters, who can be arranged through a coordinator. Before you travel:

- send recent test results, imaging (on CD or download link) and a medication list
- write down allergies and previous surgeries in English
- ask which documents you need for insurance claims at home (receipts, diagnosis certificate, English medical certificate)

## Step 6: Plan the trip around recovery

![Seoul Forest, an easy walk for recovery days](https://tong.visitkorea.or.kr/cms/resource_photo/99/3580599_image2_1.jpg "Korea Tourism Organization")

Book a stay close to the hospital for the first nights, keep the day after a procedure free, and choose light activities — a walk along [Cheonggyecheon Stream](/{lang}/concierge#spots), a café in Bukchon or a palace visit — rather than long day trips. Our [concierge page](/{lang}/concierge) lists hotels and Korean restaurants near each hospital area.

## Step 7: Payment, aftercare and if something goes wrong

Most hospitals accept international credit cards; some ask for a deposit. Keep every receipt and the itemized bill. Before you leave, make sure you have a follow-up plan, a contact at the hospital and your medical records in English.

If you have a dispute about treatment, Korea has a public mediation body, the Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency. For emergencies dial **119**; for multilingual travel help call the **1330** Korea Travel Hotline.

## FAQ

### Do I need a special visa for medical treatment in Korea?

Not always. Short procedures and screenings can usually be done on a regular visit or visa waiver. For long or repeated treatment, apply for a medical tourism visa (C-3-3, up to 90 days) or a G-1-10 visa, and check current rules for your nationality.

### Are prices in Korea the same for foreigners and Koreans?

Hospitals publish their self-pay prices to HIRA, but the price for international patients can differ, for example because of coordination or interpretation services. Always ask for a written quote and compare it with the published price.

### How do I know a hospital is allowed to treat foreign patients?

Providers that attract international patients must register under Korea's Medical Service Act. You can search registered institutions on the Medical Korea portal and confirm with the hospital's international center.

### Can I use my home health insurance?

Korean national health insurance does not cover short-term visitors, so you pay as a self-pay patient. Some international or travel insurance plans reimburse treatment abroad — check with your insurer and ask the hospital for English receipts and certificates.
